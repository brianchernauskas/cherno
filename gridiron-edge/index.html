3 · Which bet should I take? all six contracts on this game, side by side

A game gives you six ways in — either moneyline, either spread, over or under. They are not interchangeable: the same opinion priced as a moneyline and as a spread can differ by several points of vig, and a −450 favourite pays so little that being right barely matters. This ranks all six at the best price any of your books is showing, and picks the one that best fits the kind of bet you want.

Best available prices

This ranks bets by how far a book's price sits from the sharp consensus — not by who is likely to win. It uses no projection of your own. For every game it de-vigs the median of all other books, backs out the number that implies, then re-prices each book at its own line. A stale or generous book surfaces as +EV. Needs at least 4 books on a market to mean anything.

A team's spread and moneyline are two prices for the same opinion, so each team gets one row: whichever contract has the bigger edge. Other way shows the alternative's best price, edge and win rate. A higher win rate is not a better bet — the edge lives in the contract a book mispriced.

Only this week's games are ranked. The odds feed returns everything with a price up, which by Sunday already includes next Thursday; a book sitting off consensus eight days out has usually just not sharpened the number yet, and it will move several times before you could bet it. Read this before betting off it A price better than consensus is a real edge, but a small one — expect 1–3%, not 20%. It also decays fast: stale lines get corrected, often within minutes. And a single book alone against the field is sometimes right rather than slow, especially on news the others haven't priced yet. Check why it's an outlier before you fire.
Player props

The same stale-line hunt as the Best Board, applied to stat Over/Unders. For each player it de-vigs every book's Over and Under, backs out the average each price implies, takes the median of the other books, and re-prices the book under test at its own line. No projection of your own.

Props are the softest market and the most expensive to bet. Hold runs 7–10% against about 4.5% on a spread, so the minimum edge is higher here. Books also copy each other's feeds, so a flag is a lead to check against injury news, not a verdict. Props bill per game, so you load them game by game; anytime-TD is left out because a Yes-only price cannot be de-vigged. College football props exist for few games.

Weekly slate planner

Every stake elsewhere in this app is sized as though that bet is your only exposure. It isn't. This sizes the whole card at once — solving for the stakes that maximise expected log growth across all of them together — and resolves bets on the same game from a single simulated game, so a team's spread and its moneyline correlate properly instead of pretending to be independent. Uses your bankroll and Kelly fraction from the Calibrator tab.

Closing line value

Results can't tell you whether the board works for a very long time: at a 2% edge, profit takes thousands of bets to separate from luck. The closing price can. It is the market's best estimate once all the news is in, so a bet priced better than the no-vig closing consensus had a real edge whatever happened in the game. Log the bets you actually place, capture the consensus shortly before kickoff, and each bet is re-priced at your line and price against that close.

Any normal fetch updates the reads for free. A capture pulls only the games and markets you have bets on, from a fixed list of ten books anchored by Pinnacle, so one kickoff window costs a credit or two per league. With cloud tracking set up on the Settings tab, captures and grading run while this page is closed. A read counts as a close only if it was taken within 3 hours of kickoff.

Odds API

Uses the-odds-api.com. The free tier is 500 credits/month, and the /odds endpoint bills credits = markets × regions — so three markets across US books costs 3 credits per league, not 1. Naming books instead of regions bills every 10 books as one region, which is how Your books + Pinnacle gets the sharpest consensus for the least. Fetch only the markets you actually price. Your key is stored in this browser's localStorage only; it is never sent anywhere except the odds API itself.

Making 500 credits last a season Slates are cached in this browser, so reopening the app or reloading the page costs nothing — only "Fetch live odds" spends credits. Pull a slate once, work the whole card off it, and refresh only when you need current numbers. Cost is markets × regions per league, where 10 named books count as one region: all three markets on your books + Pinnacle (up to 10 books) is 6 credits for both leagues, about 83 full scans a month. Dropping moneyline makes it 4, but the board can then no longer compare a team's spread against its moneyline.
Cloud tracking captures closes and grades games while this page is closed

Auto-capture and auto-grade only run while this page is open. Cloud tracking hands both to a scheduled GitHub Action in a private repo. This page writes the list of games with open or ungraded bets — ids, kickoff times and markets, no stakes or books. The Action pulls closing odds shortly before each kickoff and final scores afterwards, and this page prices and grades them the next time it opens.

Setup is in the README: a private repo holding cloud/tracker.mjs and the workflow, your Odds API key as its ODDS_API_KEY Actions secret, and a fine-grained token with Contents: read and write on that one repo only. The token is kept in this browser's storage, which other GitHub Pages sites on the same github.io domain can also read — scope it to nothing else.

Why exchanges need a fee model An event contract carries no vig, so de-vigging one finds almost no hold — because there isn't any. The cost arrives as a separate trading fee instead. Left unmodelled, every Kalshi price would look like a free 2–3% edge and swamp the board. At k = 0.07 the fee peaks near 1.75¢ per contract at even money, still well inside a sportsbook's hold. Verify the current figure against your platform — Polymarket and Novig default to zero here.
What the sharp weight does Sharp books count this many times a retail book when the consensus is formed. At 4, two sharp books outweigh seven retail ones — which is deliberate, since they make the number rather than copy it, but it does mean the consensus becomes close to “what Pinnacle and LowVig think.” Drop it toward 2 to let the wider market have a say, or to 1 for a flat median.
Why these differ College games have fatter margin tails and a softer market, so a situational read is worth more points there. Don't raise the max lean much past these values — that's how a discipline tool turns into a rationalization tool.

Gridiron Edge is a decision-support tool. It measures whether a price beats your own projection — it does not predict outcomes, and its most common correct answer is "pass."
